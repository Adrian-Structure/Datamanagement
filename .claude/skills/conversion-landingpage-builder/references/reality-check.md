# Reality check — does the 7-section formula hold on real homepages?

The 7-section, single-CTA formula in this skill was checked against five real homepages
before being trusted: Windows (live-fetched), Apple, Tesla, Mercedes-Benz, Nvidia
(assessed from stable, well-documented public knowledge of these sites — not live-fetched;
their domains blocked automated fetching in the environment this check was run in, so
re-verify live if a real decision depends on current detail).

## Findings

None of the five follow the formula literally:

- **No problem/pain-point section.** Established brands don't open with "you're struggling
  with X" — visitors already know why they might want a phone, a car, an OS or a GPU. The
  problem/solution section only earns its place when the brand is unfamiliar and needs to
  prove relevance first.
- **Multiple CTAs, not one.** Every product/segment block carries its own CTA (Windows:
  "Explore Snap layouts", "Shop PCs", "Explore Windows security" — 5+ distinct links, plus
  6 more "Shop now" cards; the pattern repeats on Apple/Tesla/Mercedes/Nvidia with one CTA
  per product line). The "single CTA" rule is correct only for a single-offer page —
  it is the wrong rule for a page representing many offers at once.
- **Testimonials, if present, are sparse and informal.** Windows shows exactly one
  social-quote, no title/company triad. None of the five run 3 corporate testimonials with
  name+title+company on the homepage — that pattern shows up on unfamiliar-brand SaaS
  pages that must manufacture trust a known brand doesn't need.
- **No SaaS-style pricing tiers on the homepage.** Pricing (where shown at all, e.g. one
  Surface Pro price on Windows) is a single reference point, not a 2-4 tier comparison
  table — tier comparisons live on a dedicated pricing/product page, not the hub.
- **Two patterns the original formula doesn't cover, seen on Windows:** a **competitive
  comparison table** (us vs. alternatives, on named criteria) and an **FAQ accordion**.
  Both are legitimate additions for categories with active competitive pressure or
  common pre-purchase objections.
- **What does hold across all of them:** a strong visual/emotional hero, one clear CTA
  per distinct product or topic block, and a conventional footer (nav links, legal,
  social icons).

## The resulting rule — scope of this skill

This skill's 7-section/single-CTA formula is built for **a single-offer conversion page
for a brand the visitor doesn't already trust** (free-trial signup, demo request, one
product, cold or lukewarm traffic). It is the right tool for exactly that job.

It is the **wrong** tool, unmodified, for a **multi-product hub homepage of an established
brand** (the visitor already trusts the brand and is choosing between several of its
offers). For that case:

- Drop the single-CTA rule; use one CTA per distinct product/segment block instead.
- Drop the problem/solution section; open directly with the strongest visual + a short
  aspirational line.
- Testimonials and tiered pricing are optional, not required — include only if the brand
  is still building trust in that category.
- Consider a comparison table and/or FAQ accordion instead, if the category has active
  competition or common objections — these are proven real-world patterns, not filler.

When a request is ambiguous between the two (e.g. "make our SaaS homepage"), ask which job
the page has to do — win one conversion from a cold visitor, or route an already-interested
visitor to the right offer among several — before picking the structure.
